# Research — Dinner sweet runs out for late-coming girls in our hostel mess

**By:** Esha Panwar, M.Tech, IIIT Allahabad
**Date:** 20 November 2026
**Place:** [Hostel name] girls' mess, IIIT Allahabad (Jhalwa campus)

## The problem (5 lines)
In our [hostel name] girls' mess, the sweet dish at dinner sometimes finishes early, and girls who come late get none.
On other days, the same sweet is left over and wasted.
The mess makes roughly the same quantity every day, and the serving size (ladles/pieces per girl) is not adjusted while dinner is going on.
So the problem is not only how much is made, but how it is served through the evening — it is unfair to late-comers and wasteful.
Tonight's sweet is custard, which is served by ladle, so the serving size can vary a lot from one server to another.

## Sources I actually read
1. **Assessment of Food Wastage in Hostel Messes – An Exploratory Qualitative Study** — M. Rajalakshmi & S. Harshavardhini, Journal of Medical Evidence, 2025
   https://journals.lww.com/10.4103/JME.JME_118_24
   - The researchers talked to 50 students from girls' and boys' hostels to find out why food gets wasted in the mess.
   - They found three kinds of reasons: the students' own habits, the mess staff, and the management/warden.
   - This told me the mess staff side matters too, not just the students — for my problem, how the servers give out the sweet is part of the cause.

2. **Going zero waste in canteens: Exploring food demand with data analytics** — D. X. R. Pereira, University of Porto, 2018
   https://core.ac.uk/download/160239158.pdf
   - A student at the University of Porto tried to predict how many meals their canteen would serve each day, using two years of data.
   - The predictions would have led to about five times less food waste than the canteen manager's own guesses.
   - This shows that even simple numbers from past days beat guessing — but our mess does not have two years of data, so my tool has to work with a few weeks.
